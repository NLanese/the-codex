## BASIC RULES AND OBJECTIVE: ##

Players take turns controlliong their miniature robots. 
Each turn, players use every card in their hand to move, attack, or purchase better gear from the armory. 
Using certain cards, players will be able to damage other robots, thus taking the token and claiming it as a victory point for themselves. 
Robots have a finite amount of health tokens, whenever a player's robot runs out of health tokens, that robot is removed from the board. 
When a robot is 'knocked out' and removed, it returns to the board on its next turn, and refills its health tokens from the pool of remaining health tokens. 
The player will fill all but one of their available health points with a red token, and the final health point with a blue token.
**The game ends when a player who has been knocked out and is set ot return to the game board, but cannot since there are no blue health tokens remaining.** 
At this point, the player with the most victory points wins!

The rules say "Whoever built something" most recently goes first...
 but that's pretty weird. So we say you all roll a die and whoever rolls lowest goes first. 
From there, turns go in clockwise order

## PLAYER TURNS: ##
- Turn Structure:
    A Player's turn is broken up into the following phases...
    - Start of Turn Phase: If you have any “at the start of tum”
      effects, apply them. For example, if you are on any of the 9 spaces
      in the center of the Game Board, you gain 1 Victory Point. If there
      is currently a tile over the space, do not gain the 1 Victory Point.
    - Respawn Phase: If you have been Knocked Out, put your robot
      onto any unoccupied spawn tile and refill their Health. 
    - Main (Action) Phase:  You may play cards, use abilities, spend Energy
      to buy cards, and spend Mave to move your robot or push other
      robots. You may do these as many times as you want to and in any
      order you choose.
    - End of Turn Phase: If you have any 'at the end of your turn' effects, 
      apply them now
    - Cleanup Phase: Put any cards you have in play into your
      discard pile, then discard any cards left in your hand, then draw
      5 new cards. You lose any unspent Energy and Movement.

    During your turn, you may play cards from your hand one at a time.
    Cards are free to play (you only pay costs when buying cards, see
    the next section). When you play a card, put it “in play” next to your
    Player Board, then do what the card says. At the end of your turn,
    put any cards you have in play into your discard pile and discard any
    cards left in your hand.
    A player's hand will consist of 5 cards, unless otherwise specified. 
    Each turn a player will discard the entire hand, whether or not a card is used. 
    This means to say there is no strategic advantage in not using a card; unless that cards usage would actively hard you.

    Each card has its own abilities; and there are several different kinds of cards which we will cover below...

    - Battery Cards: 
        Batteries give you a specified amount of energy. 
        Energy is used to either move your robot (one energy moves your robot one tile; 
        and robots CANNOT move diagonally unless otherwise specified)
        or to make a purchase from the store. Items in the store have their 'energy price' shown in
        the top right corner of the card. It takes that many energy to purchase this card. THIS IS NOT 
        A MARKER OF HOW MUCH ENERGY IT TAKES TO USE THIS CARD ONCE ACQUIRED. For example, the Rocket Punch card is worth
        3 energy. If I ahd three batteries in my hand, I could purchase this card. Once I have purchased it, 
        I can use it without expending any energy the next time I get that card in my hand. We will cover more on 
        purchase later. 

        There are multiple kinds of batteries. 
        Advanced Batteries are worth 3 energy, while regular batteries are worth 1. 
        There are other ways to get energy, but batteries are the most regular and most reliable. 
        It is a good strategy to try to acquire advanced batteries early in the game so that you can 
        purchase higher level gear more quickly. 

    - Function Cards:
        Function Cards are the purple ones, and they are some of the most interesting in the game.
        Most function cards enable different movement, better attack range, or even immediate acquisition of Victory Points.
        Some will stay active, but most only have one-turn long durations. 
        You cna play these cards whenever you like during the turn, and you have until the end of your turn to 
        take the benefit of the card. This means to say when you use the card, you do NOT need to do everything in the Effect area right away.

    - Attack Cards:
        Attack Cards are a player's way of damaging others. They do not require energy to use once purchased. 
        Every attack card will have a 'damage' and a 'range' value along the left hand side, beneath the purchase price. 
        - Damage is how many health tokens / victory points you take from the player that was damaged. 
        - Range indicates how many tiles away the other robot can be in order for your attack to hit. If the number is 1,
          the enemy must be one tile away non diagonally. 
          If the number is 2, the enemy can have a tile in between the attacking player and him/herself and still be damaged. 
          Ranged attacks can never go diagonally unless otherwise specified by the attack card or a function card. 
        - Sometimes, the card will not have a range icon and number, and will instead have a boxing glove icon to indicate this is a melee attack.
          Melee Attacks will always have a range of one, but they ARE able to hit opponents diagonally.

HEALTH (..°), DAMAGE (+=), AND VICTORY POINTS (> )

Cubes on a Player Board represent a robot's Health. Cubes ina
Victory Point Pile represent Victory Points. Robots start the game
with and respawn with full Health (fill their Health bar by putting
one blue cube ona 7 and red cubes on the rest).

Damage causes robots to lose Health. Whenever a robat loses their
last Health cube, they are Knocked Out (see the next section).
Any extra damage has no effect.

Whenever an enemy robot loses Health on your turn for any reason
(for example, if you attack them or push them onto the Tacks tile),
they lose that many Health cubes, removing them from their Player
Board. You claim those cubes as Victory Points and add them te
your Victory Point Pile. Red cubes are removed first and are worth
1 point each. Blue cubes are removed last. Blue cubes on Player
Boards represent 1 Health each, however, blue cubes in Victory
Point Piles count as 2 Victory Points each!

Whenever you or another friendly rabot loses Health on your turn
(for example, if you willingly move onto the Tacks tile), you a/so lose
Victory Points. When you lose a red Health cube you lose 1 Victory
Paint, and when you lose a blue Health cube you lose 2 Victory
Points. All red cubes (Health and Victory Points) lost this way go
back to the Reserve. All blue cubes lost this way are returned to
the Game Box (never return blue cubes to the Reserve).

When an effect repairs Health (of), take that many red cubes
from the Reserve and add them to your Player Board. A robot
cannot exceed full Health.

When an effect gives you Victory Paints ( ig ), claim that many
red cubes from the Reserve and add them to your Victory Point Pile.

GETTING KNOCKED OUT AND RESPAWNING

Whenever a robot loses their last Health cube, they are Knocked Out
and temporarily removed from the Game Board. If an effect says
to Knock Out a robot, they also lose all of their Health.

If your robot gets Knoeked Out, during your next Respawn Phase,
place your robot on any uncecupied spawn tile. Then refill their
Health using cubes from the Reserve (one .“ gets a blue cube and
the rest get red cubes).

Note: Whenever a robot respawns, their “start of turn” effects
do not trigger that turn (for example, if Pug spawns on one
of the 9 spaces in the center of the Game Board).

DRAWING CARDS AND CLEANUP

At the end of each of your turns, you will discard your hand and
draw 5 cards from your personal deck.

If you would draw a card from your personal deck but it is empty,
shuffle your discard pile to create a new personal deck, then finish
drawing. Fer example, it is the Cleanup Phase and you need to draw
five cards, but you only have three cards left in your personal deck.
So, you draw these three cards, shuffle your discard pile to create
a new personal deck, then finish drawing two more cards.

When a robot would respawn but there are ne blue Health cubes
left in the Reserve, the game ends.

Players count the Vietory Points in their Victery Point Piles.
Each red cube is worth 1 point, each blue cube is worth 2 points,
and each gem is worth 5 points. Do not count any cubes on
Player Boards. The player with the mest Victory Points wins!

In the event of a tie, the player with the most Health cubes on
their Player Board wins. Otherwise, the victory is shared.

2-PLAYER RULES

In a 2 player game, all rules remain the same except:

- Each player gets 2 Player Boards at the start of
the game and controls both of their robots with
a single personal deck. Your Energy and Nove are
shared between your robots.

» When you play a card, you play it for one of
your robots, not both. If it refers to you or your
robot, it is referring to the robot you played it
for. If it is an Attack card, its range
is relative to the robot you played it for.

- Ifa tile refers to you or your robot, it is referring
to the robot on that tile.

Robot Quest Arena can also be played with even
teams. All rules remain the same except:

- Players take their turns in the following order:
a. Team A’s Player 1 takes turn 1
b. Team B’s Player 1 takes turn 2
c. Team A’s Player 2 takes turn 3
d. Team B’s Player 2 takes turn 4
e. Repeat

» Remember, if your teammate loses Health
during your turn, you (not your teammate)
also lose that many Victory Points.

- At the end of the game, players on the same
team combine their Victory Points.
